Method

How the desk measures the day, and how the strategies are tested.

The unit: range still to come

A futures day runs from 18:00 to 17:00 New York time, and the MyFundedFutures account Iron Brain is fitted to is flattened at 16:10. Each market’s typical daily range is tracked with a 20-day exponential average. The volatility profile measures how much of a day’s movement each hour usually carries, so at any moment the engine knows how much range is still to come before the flat. Ladder levels are fractions of that remaining range.

Touch odds

For every hour of every past day, the engine measured how far price went above and below that hour’s close before 16:00, in remaining-range units, separately for up and down moves. Those distributions become the odds for any level you check. Late in the day, with only a bar or two left, moves fall short of what a single distribution expects, so each hour can get its own (pooled with the hours either side), used only where that beats the shared version out of sample.

Pace

A fast day tends to stay fast. Today’s range so far, compared with what the profile expected by now, stretches or shrinks the remaining range. It is used only because it beat the plain model out of sample in both halves of the test.

Strategy lab

Every strategy was registered with fixed rules before it was run, and each is tested on both markets. Intraday rules decide on completed hourly bars, enter at the next bar’s open, check stops before targets inside a bar and are flat at the 16:00 bar close. Daily rules trade close to close; they hold overnight, which the 50K Builder doesn’t allow, so they’re kept as research. Costs are about one tick of slippage each side plus commission.

To pass, a rule needs a t-statistic past one Bonferroni bar across every test, must beat 95% of 200 runs of the same rule at random times, and must be profitable in both halves of its history and over the last year. Each test also reports the smallest edge its data could detect, so a rule that couldn’t be judged is labelled inconclusive rather than failed. Trades after each rule’s registration date form a forward record it could not have been tuned on.

The evaluation simulation

For each rule the account allows, the lab replays the evaluation from the rule’s own history: micros sized at the rule’s stop, the $1,000 daily soft pause, and the max loss trailing the best end-of-day balance, over 60 trading days. The pass rate sits next to a luck baseline, the same trades with their average profit removed, because with a $3,000 target and a $2,000 drawdown even a coin flip passes fairly often.
