Method

The unit: expected remaining range

A trading day runs 18:00 to 17:00 New York time. Each market’s typical daily range is tracked with a 20-day exponential average. The volatility profile measures how much of a day’s movement each hour usually carries, so at any moment the engine knows how much range is still to come. Levels are expressed as fractions of that remaining range.

Touch odds

For every hour of every past day, the engine measured how far price went above and below that hour’s close before 17:00, in remaining-range units. Those distributions, kept separately for up and down moves, become the odds for any level you check.

Pace

A fast day tends to stay fast. Today’s range so far, compared with what the profile expected by now, stretches or shrinks the remaining range. This adjustment is used only because it beat the plain model out of sample in both halves of the test.

What failed

No hour of the day closes up more often than chance once you account for testing every hour in both markets. The desk shows those tests but leans on none of them. The opening-range statistics are still collecting: there are only about 60 days of free 5-minute bars, and they need 250 before they count.

Live Bridge

The optional Chrome extension reads the symbol and last price from your open TradingView tab titles and feeds them to this page, so the odds use the live price. It reads nothing else and sends nothing anywhere. To install it, load the live-bridge folder from the repository at chrome://extensions with Developer mode on.
